An intergalactic dice game of fleet management

Space Base

You are the commodore of a small fleet, a ship stationed at each of the twelve sectors of your console. Every turn two dice are rolled, and every commodore at once takes either each die’s sector or the sector of their sum: the one who rolled from the ships stationed there, everyone else from the ships they have deployed. Buy new ships with your credits — it spends them all — and the ships they replace go out to their sectors to pay you on everyone else’s turns. Cargo vessels bring credits, mining vessels income, carriers victory points; charge up the command ships for rerolls, set dice and swapped sectors, settle colonies, and be the first past 40. Two to five players, peer-to-peer: no accounts, no server, just a room code. Bots fill any empty seat.

    How to play Space Base

    1. The goal. Be the commodore with the most victory points (VP) once someone has reached 40 — the round is then played out, so everyone has had as many turns.
    2. Your console has twelve sectors, numbered like the dice can make them, with a ship stationed at each. Ships you replace are deployed: tucked under the console at the same sector, where only their red, deployed reward still shows. A sector holds one stationed ship and any number of deployed ones. You keep track of ¢ credits, i income and V VP.
    3. A turn. The active player rolls both dice. Then every player, each for themselves, takes either each die’s sector or the sector of their sum. The active player gains the blue station reward of each sector taken; everyone else gains the red rewards of every ship deployed there. Doubles taken separately pay that sector twice.
    4. Buy one card, if you like: any of the six face-up ships of each level in the shipyard, or a colony, that costs no more than your credits. Buying spends all your credits, whatever the card costs — the only way to save is not to buy. The ship is stationed at its sector and the one there is deployed beneath it.
    5. Income. At the end of your turn, credits below your income rise to it. The shipyard is refilled to six a level.
    6. Arrows pay out the next sector along as well — the one the arrow points to, or either one for a two-way arrow. In one die’s activation a card pays once, so arrows never loop.
    7. Charge squares. A ship with squares gains a charge cube instead (and any reward printed with the squares). Blue squares fill on your turn, red ones on the others’. Linked squares must all be filled, and the ability spends them all; separate squares are a reserve, one cube a use. A square with pips counts only at the table sizes its pips show.
    8. Abilities are blue (your turn only), red (the other players’ turns) or green (anyone’s turn). Most may be used before the roll or after the rewards: credits, extra buys and free claims, swapping every card of two sectors, placing a charge, exchanging two ships, and the Armstrong’s outright win. Setting a die comes before the roll, a reroll right after it, and the dice-and-arrows ships raise the sum as you take it; a 2× ship doubles one sector you take.
    9. Colonies score their VP at once and close their sector for good: nothing more is stationed or deployed there and it pays nothing on your rolls — though the ships deployed there still pay on others’.
    10. The end. When someone reaches 40 VP the round is finished: the player to the right of the starting player has the last turn. Most VP wins; on a tie, one more round is played, and if still tied the victory is shared.
    11. The start. Everyone has 5 credits, draws a level 1 ship and buys it. The highest sector drawn goes first; the 2nd player gains 1 credit, the 3rd 2 credits, the 4th and 5th 1 income each. Light-Speed (a lobby option): 15 credits and 1 income each, and four level 1 and two level 2 ships to buy from; whoever keeps the most credits goes first.

    At this table

    • Taking the dice. Every way you could take them is worked out and offered as what it would bring you. When one way is as good as or better than every other in everything, it is taken for you and the table does not wait. Which way a two-way arrow points, which charged ships raise the sum and whether to double a sector are part of the choice.
    • Abilities between turns. The green and red abilities of the players who are not rolling can be used while the active player has yet to roll, and while they buy; between the roll and the rewards the consoles stand still.
    • Small rulings. The ship you buy “into any sector 7–12” is a ship, not a colony; the exchange never moves a colony; a charge placed “anywhere” goes on another of your ships, in a square that counts. The box’s 30 charge cubes are not a limit here.

    Space Base is designed by John D. Clair and published by Alderac Entertainment Group (AEG). This is an unofficial fan implementation. The rules are AEG’s Revised Field Manual, with its first Field Manual for the finer points; where the two differ the revision is followed — a tie is played off with one more round, then shared. The cards’ costs, sectors and rewards are the licensed online edition’s, checked against pictures of every card; their charge squares are the Field Manual’s own pictures wherever it shows a card. The box gives each player one of five differently named sets of starting ships; every console here carries the set the Field Manual pictures.
