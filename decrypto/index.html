DECRYPTO

Your team shares four secret keywords. Each round, one of you transmits a 3-digit code with three clues — clear enough for your teammates, cryptic enough that the other team can't crack the pattern. Intercept twice to win; garble your own transmission twice and you lose. Two teams of 2–4, peer-to-peer: no accounts, no server, just a room code.

      How to play Decrypto

      1. Teams: White and Black, 2–4 players each. Each team sees its own four numbered keywords — fixed for the whole game, hidden from the enemy.
      2. Each round, both teams transmit: the round's encryptor secretly draws a 3-digit code (three different digits from 1–4) and gives 3 clues, one per digit, each pointing at the keyword in that position. Codes can repeat between rounds.
      3. Everyone hears every clue. The encryptor's own team locks in a guess to decrypt it. From Round 2 on, the enemy team also locks an interception guess first.
      4. Tokens: a wrong guess by the encryptor's own team = a ⚫ miscommunication. A correct enemy interception = a ⚪ interception for them (a wrong interception costs nothing).
      5. Winning: 2 ⚪ tokens win the game; 2 ⚫ tokens lose it. Checked at the end of each round — rounds always finish. 8 rounds max.
      6. Ties (both end in the same round, or no decision after 8 rounds): count points (⚪ +1, ⚫ −1). Still tied? Each team guesses the other's four keywords — most correct wins; still tied is a shared victory.
      7. Clue rules: clues refer to the meaning of a keyword — never its spelling, length, position, or sound. Public knowledge only. A clue can be a word or a whole phrase, but you may never reuse a clue, and never use a keyword itself.
      8. Table discipline: plan in team chat — the enemy reads everything you say out loud (the all-chat). The encryptor keeps a straight face and never comments on guesses.

      Decrypto is by Thomas Dagenais-Lespérance, published by Le Scorpion Masqué. This is an unofficial fan implementation for playing with friends; the 440-word keyword pool is our own.
