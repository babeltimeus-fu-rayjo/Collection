The Resistance

AVALON

Arthur’s knights ride out on five Quests — and hidden among them are the Minions of Mordred. Each round a Leader picks a Team, the whole table votes on it in the open, and the Team plays its Quest cards face down: a single Fail and the Quest is lost. Merlin knows the traitors but cannot say so; if Good wins three Quests, the Assassin gets one guess at who he is. Five to ten players, peer-to-peer: no accounts, no server, just a room code. Bots fill any empty seat.

    How to play Avalon

    1. Two sides. Every player is dealt a secret Character: a Loyal Servant of Arthur (Good) or a Minion of Mordred (Evil). At 5 players it is 3 Good against 2 Evil; at 6, 4–2; at 7, 4–3; at 8, 5–3; at 9, 6–3; at 10, 6–4. The agents of Evil know each other.
    2. Merlin and the Assassin are in every game. Merlin, on the side of Good, sees the agents of Evil — but if Good wins, the Assassin gets one chance to name him, and naming him wins it for Evil. So Merlin can only hint.
    3. Team Building. Each round the Leader picks a Team for the next Quest — two to five players, as the Quest track shows. The Leader may pick themselves, or not.
    4. The vote. Everyone, the Leader too, votes to approve or reject the Team; the votes turn over together. A majority approves it and it goes on the Quest; a tie or less rejects it, and the next player clockwise becomes Leader. If five Teams are rejected in a single round, Evil wins.
    5. The Quest. Each member of the Team plays Success or Fail, face down. Good must play Success; Evil may play either. A single Fail fails the Quest — except the 4th Quest at 7 or more players, which needs two. Only the number of Fails is ever shown.
    6. Winning. Three failed Quests and Evil wins. Three successful Quests, and the Assassin names Merlin: if right, Evil wins; if not, Good does.
    7. Optional Characters. Percival (Good) knows Merlin. Morgana (Evil) appears to Percival as Merlin, so he sees two and cannot tell which. Mordred (Evil) is hidden from Merlin. Oberon (Evil) does not know the other agents of Evil, nor they him — but Merlin sees him.
    8. The Lady of the Lake (optional, best at 7 or more). She starts with the player on the first Leader’s right. After the 2nd, 3rd and 4th Quests, her holder secretly sees one player’s loyalty — anyone who has not held her — may tell the table anything about it, and passes her to that player.
    9. Talk. “Players may make any claims during the game, at any point in the game.” Use the chat: accuse, defend, bluff. Point at or tap anything at the table to see what it means; the vote record on the side keeps every Team and every vote.

    The Resistance: Avalon is by Don Eskridge, published by Indie Boards & Cards. This is an unofficial fan implementation. The rules, the Set Up chart (the sides at each table size) and the Team Building chart (the Team for each Quest) follow the publisher’s rulebook, as do the reveal scripts that decide who sees whom.

    Keep it to yourself — “A player’s Character Card may not be revealed at any point in the game.”
